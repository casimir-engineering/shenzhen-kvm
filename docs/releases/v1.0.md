- One keyboard, mouse and clipboard for your Mac and a Linux computer on the same network.
- Link the two computers in the app: make one visible, choose it on the other, compare a six-digit code.
- Updates itself: Shenzhen KVM checks once a day and asks before installing.

---

## Shenzhen KVM 1.0

### Keyboard and mouse
- **One pointer for both computers.** It's on one screen at a time, and both keyboards and mice act there, from wherever it is. Push it past the side of your screens where the other computer is, with either mouse, and it carries on there; the other screen hides its pointer. Both Shift keys bring it back to the computer whose keyboard you're using.
- **Both ways.** The Linux computer's keyboard and mouse control the Mac too. Each direction can be switched off.
- **Shortcuts act where the pointer is.** ⌘Tab, ⌘Space, Mission Control keys and screenshots go to the other computer while the pointer is there. On Linux, ⌘ acts as Control (⌘C copies), or as Super if you choose; ⌘Tab switches windows there. Volume and playback keys follow the pointer; brightness stays on the Mac.

### Clipboard
- **Copy on one, paste on the other**, with the usual shortcuts: text, pictures, files and folders. Files go in the background with their progress in the window, and a paste waits for them. They land in Downloads, in a folder named after the other computer.
- You can keep files over a size limit from being sent, or use two dedicated shortcuts instead of the usual copy and paste.

### Linking
- **No addresses to type.** Make one computer visible for 5 minutes, choose it on the other, and check that both show the same code. The two then find each other by themselves, also when their addresses change.
- **Found on any ordinary network.** The computers look for each other in several ways at once, so office Wi-Fi that blocks one of them still works. A VPN is never used for this.
- **Forget** in either window unlinks both computers.

### The Linux computer
- Install it from the repository (`linux/install.sh`). It adds a keyboard icon to the top bar, with the same menu and window as the Mac, and the `shenzhen-kvm` command.

## Installing

Download `ShenzhenKVM.dmg`, open it and drag Shenzhen KVM onto Applications. When you first open it, macOS asks before it can use the keyboard and mouse: choose **Open System Settings** and turn on Shenzhen KVM under Accessibility. Shenzhen KVM then starts by itself when you log in.

For a smoother pointer over Wi-Fi, choose **Set Up…** next to "Smooth pointer" in the window; it asks for your Mac's password once.
